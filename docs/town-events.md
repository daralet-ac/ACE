# Town events

Players questing and hunting around a town stir up unrest there. Once a town's unrest passes its threshold, the town may come under attack: waves of creatures from its own region march in along its roads, and players band together with the town guards in a Town Defense fellowship to hold it. The full design is in the Town Event System design doc; this is the first version (the doc's MVP phase).

The code is in `apps/server/TownEvents/`: `TownEventManager` (unrest, triggering, the hook queue), `TownEvent` (one attack), `TownGeography` (regions and roads) and `TownCreaturePool` (which creatures a town sends).

## Turning it on

`town_events_enabled` (off by default) lets unrest start attacks. `@townevent start <town>` starts one whether or not it is on, for testing.

## Commands

| Command | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| `@townevent start <town> [chest \| nochest]` | Starts an attack on the town now; `chest` makes it a raid on the town's Supply Chest, `nochest` rules one out |
| `@townevent stop <town>` | Calls it off: the attackers vanish and the Town Defense disbands |
| `@townevent status` | Each attack's phase, wave and attackers, and every player's score, over-level kills included |
| `@townevent unrest [<town> <amount>]` | Every town's unrest against its threshold, or sets one town's |
| `@townevent supply [<town> [<amount> \| level <level>]]` | Every supplied town's guard supply, one town's in full, or sets one town's (to an amount, or to the start of a level) with no minimum hold |
| `@townevent history [<town>] [<count>]` | The latest attacks from the event log, newest first (10, up to 50) |
| `@adminui townevents` | The admin plugin's Town Events tab: unrest, attacks and scores, every 2 seconds (Admin) |
| `@fakeplayer add [<count>] [<level>] \| list \| join \| leave [<count>] \| remove [all \| <number>]` | Fake players for testing the Town Defense and wave sizes (`FakePlayerManager`): real players in front of you with no client, which count as online and eligible, say yes to the join prompt, are never removed for not fighting, and can't be attacked. They are never saved (character, biota or items), leave no offline record, are left out of the event log, and go when removed, booted or the server shuts down |
| `/towndefense join` | Joins the Town Defense of the town the player is in (for one who said no to the prompt, or was removed) |

## How an attack runs

1. **Unrest.** A player's kill of a creature at or above the nearest town's tier adds 1 (0.5 in a dungeon), a quest stamp 2, and town attunement progress (`Quest{Town}`) 25. Unrest decays by 0.25 a minute. Every town's unrest, threshold roll and cooldown are kept in `town_events_unrest` (JSON, written once a minute and saved with the other properties), so a restart doesn't reset them. A town's threshold is rolled between the base threshold (`town_events_base_threshold`, 150 by default, also set from the admin plugin's Town Events tab) and double it, after each attack; changing the base counts at once. Once past it, the town has a chance a minute of an attack, 10% at the threshold and climbing in a straight line to 50% at double it, if someone at its level (or Shrouded) is in its region, it isn't cooling down (45 minutes after an attack), and fewer than 2 attacks are on.
2. **Preparing.** The town's creature pool is read from the world database: every creature the landblock instances and encounters within 3 landblocks spawn (through their generators) that uses the archetype system, attacks on sight and is of the town's tier. The roads in come from the town's patrolling guards: each patrol route's waypoint farthest from the centre is an entry point, and the route walked back toward the centre is the way in. **Raid themes.** Each guarded town has a few curated kinds of raid (`TownRaidThemes`, generated by world-db's `scripts/town-guards/town_raid_themes.py` from creature names), from what lives around it and in its nearby dungeons: drudge, banderling, mosswart, tumerok, sclavus or moarsman raiders, a Lugian war band with its mattekars, the restless dead led by a lich, the mu-miyah, a Conjurer with his golems, virindi with tuskers and grievvers, shadows, zefirs, bandits. One is picked per attack. A theme is a ladder of creatures from the low tiers to the high: the town gets the ones in its level band and within 3 levels of it, or with none in it, the nearest and any within 6 more levels (`docs/town-raid-themes.md` lists what each town gets), and a creature from outside the band is scaled into it (a random level in the band, the top of it for the Warlord), setting its archetype level and tier, and moving its spells to the town's spell level (as the guards' weapon spells, I to VIII). Every attacker is a look-alike: its own emotes are cleared when it spawns, so a borrowed boss or quest creature stamps no quests, gives no rewards and counts toward no kill tallies, and a theme can borrow one under another name (a dungeon's Artificer raids as a Conjurer). The raiders are the main family; a theme's beasts or golems come as the second, smaller family (its pets); its leaders are the Warlord's candidates, or else the highest-level raider leads. A town without a theme (none of the guarded towns) uses its creature pool, where only the humanoid races, shadows, virindi and zefirs can be the main family and beasts only come as the second; with no such family it can't be attacked. In a pool raid, a main family with both casters (creatures with a spellbook) and fighters is preferred. If it has only one kind, a second, smaller family that has the other is added; otherwise 30% of attacks get a second family anyway. Each creature in a wave is a caster 35% of the time when both kinds are there, and from the main family 70% of the time. Then 2 or 3 roads are picked.
   - **Caster roles.** Every wave has a war caster and a support caster (`TownCasterKits`): the first attackers are made one of each until the wave has both, counting creatures whose own spellbook already fits, and after that 15% of attackers are made one or the other. A creature whose spellbook doesn't fit its role gets the role's spells ahead of its own, at the spell level of the town's tier (as the guards' weapon spells, I to VIII). A war caster gets a random war bolt. A support caster gets Heal Other, Aura of Blood Drinker Other and Aura of Heart Seeker Other, with Harm Other and all three Martyr's (Hecatomb, Tenacity, Blight) to fight with. Martyr's spells stop at VII. Either role has at least 1.0 archetype magic, so its spells land and it has the mana for them. An attacker that rises again (a kill that didn't count) keeps its role.
   - **Support casting** works for any monster, not just attackers: a monster with a support spell in its spellbook (a heal, a buff for another creature, a weapon aura, or a buff for a held weapon) casts it on an ally that needs it, from where it stands, and otherwise fights with the rest of its spellbook. An ally is itself or a living monster that isn't its foe by faction or creature type and isn't fighting it, in the spell's range and in sight. Heals and other boosts go to the ally lowest on that vital, once it's below 50%. Buffs go to the nearest ally without one as strong in that spell's category; weapon auras and weapon buffs need a held weapon, so they're never cast on a beast that fights with claws. A beneficial spell for someone else that it can't use this way is never cast at all, so a monster never buffs or heals its foe. Item and Creature spells, which most monsters have no skill in, use the monster's Life Magic range. So guards with Heal Other would heal each other and the Captain, never the raiders.
3. **Warning.** Everyone in the town's region, and every at-level player anywhere attuned to the town (`Attuned{Town}`), is told what's coming and from which roads. The first wave comes 2 minutes later, with reminders to the same players 1 minute and 30 seconds before it. The Town Defense opens with the warning: players in the town area get the prompt then, so defenders can group up and buff before the fighting, and the grace a new member gets to reach the fighting counts from the first wave.
4. **Waves** (2 to 4). Each wave's budget is 10 + 1 per member of the Town Defense when it spawns. A creature costs its archetype toughness × lethality, times its variant's health and lethality multipliers: Raider 1.5 × 1.25, Elite 2 × 1.5 (from wave 2). The last wave brings a Warlord (the family's highest-level creature, 4 × health and 20% more for each member of the Town Defense past the first when it spawns, 1.5 × lethality, and stronger with the town's guard supply level (Elites get 75% of this scaling and Raiders 50%; plain attackers none): per level, +20% archetype toughness, +2% physicality, dexterity, magic and intelligence, and +10% lethality, so at level 10 it has 3 × the toughness and 2 × the lethality; `TownArchetypeMods`). Attackers march in along their road and fight the members of the Town Defense, the guards and the Captain within their awareness range, then march on. Guards are ethereal (players walk through them on patrol), and the physics skips an ethereal object that ignores collisions; a projectile aimed at one still reaches it (`PhysicsObj.FindObjCollisions`), so attackers' bolts and arrows hit guards, and a sight test looks from the guard's spot back to the attacker (`WorldObject.IsDirectVisible`). A town's road can start farther out than its guards' patrol (`TownGeography.RoadLeadIns`: Qalaba'r's north-western road starts past the town sign). While a town is under attack its guards don't respawn: a dead guard's spawn waits in its generator's queue until the attack ends (`TownEventManager.HoldsGuardRespawn`; the Captain still comes back after 5 minutes). A wave ends when its attackers are dead; after 6 minutes the stragglers are removed. 25 seconds pass between waves.
5. **The end.** The town holds when the last wave is cleared. After 30 minutes the attack ends as a failure.

## The Town Defense

- When the warning goes out, every player in the town area (150 m of the centre) is asked to join, and so is anyone who arrives while it goes on. Accepting takes a player out of their own fellowship.
- Leave is the only fellowship action that works in it: Recruit, Dismiss, Disband, Assign New Leader, Change Openness and `/lfmore` are refused. It has no size cap, and disbands when the attack ends.
- If every member leaves, the next player to join founds it again (by the prompt or `/towndefense join`, which also lets the last one out back in). The town's Captain of the Guard, while alive, is shown in its panel as a fellow and as its leader, with their health, stamina and mana kept up to date (`TownEvent.TickCaptain`), so it reads as the Captain's company however often it is founded again. The Captain is only shown: the fellowship's real members, and its leader on the server, are players.
- **Only the Town Defense fights.** Attackers leave anyone outside it, and their pets, alone, and can't be harmed by them (`Creature.TownEventIgnores`, `Player.CheckPKStatusVsTarget`), so lifestones, portals and squares need no extra guards. A player outside it who attacks one is told it's an invalid target (the client's own "cannot be harmed" text is garbled) and asked to join, with the usual prompt, at most every 10 seconds. Only members are scored and rewarded.
- An over-level player who joins is Shrouded, and can't drop the Shroud while in the fellowship.
- A member away from the fight is warned and removed after 60 seconds. The fight reaches 50 m past the farthest road end attackers spawn from this attack (at least the 150 m town area), and a member who hit or healed in the last 15 seconds is never away, wherever the fight took them. A member who does nothing for 60 seconds of a wave (after a 2-minute grace on joining) is warned and removed 60 seconds later. Removal keeps their scores, but only members are rewarded, so a removed player has to rejoin.

## Scoring and rewards

- Damage dealt to attackers, life magic healing on other participants, killing blows, and damage taken from attackers before armor, resistances and shields.
- Only members at the town's level (or Shrouded) are scored. A kill by anyone else (one who left with the blow on its way, or a member over the level band whose Shroud lapsed) doesn't count: the attacker rises again at a road in, the player is prompted to join again, and an over-level kill is logged (with how often they refused).
- **Contribution** (0 to 10, `TownEvent.GetContributionMultiplier`): in each of the four categories, a player's share of the top score among those rewarded, times 10, added up and capped at 10. Topping one category is the most there is; half the top damage and nothing else is 5; half the top healing and three quarters of the top killing blows is 12.5, capped at 10. A late joiner or a light hitter gets a small share, so a small reward.
- **XP:** everyone who took part and is still in the Town Defense gets the town's base share of a level, (1 − 0.1 × tier)% (1% at tier 0: Holtburg, Shoushi, Yaraq; 0.5% at tier 5), times their contribution, so up to (10 − tier)%; then 10% more for each of the town's supply levels, and a quarter for a failed defence. A player over the town's level gets what a player at its top level would.
- **The Warlord's spoils:** if the Warlord died, by a defender or a guard, each member rewarded at the end finds on its body 50 × the town's tier (tier 0 counting as 1) × their contribution pyreals, 10% more for each supply level: up to 500 at Holtburg, 1,500 at tier 3. They're paid when the attack ends, once every score is in. A kill that doesn't count raises the Warlord again. The attack's pyreals come only from here.
- **Defense Commendation Tokens** (1057552): a held town's reward comes with one per tier of the town (none at tier 0), from the Captain with the XP. They're spent at the Commendation Quartermaster (2020060), beside the Captain in Cragstone, Hebian-To and Zaikhal (world-db `src/universal/weenies/Town Events`; each item's Value is its price in tokens, at about 1,000 pyreals a token):
  - **Supply Requisition** (1057553), 1 token: given to any town's Captain, it adds 2,000 pyreals' worth to that town's supply (`TownCaptain.SupplyPerRequisition`), twice a trade note's worth, but doesn't count toward Guard's Gratitude.
  - **Writs of Gratitude** (1057554-1057560), one per tier 0 to 6: cast Guard's Gratitude for that tier's donation minimum in tokens (1, 1, 4, 9, 16, 25, 36). A player above the tier's level band can't use it (`UseRequiresMaxLevel`, PropertyInt 538, checked in `WorldObject.CheckUseRequirements`), so they need their own tier's.
  - **Loot Crates** (1057561-1057581), Warrior, Rogue and Caster for tiers 1 to 7, 5 × tier tokens: each opens into one magic piece of armor or weapon that requires its class's attribute, Strength, Coordination or Self (treasure profiles 10059-10061, using magic item tables 31-33), at loot tier = tier + 1 and LootQualityMod 0.5. So they never hold jewelry, clothing or sigil trinkets. A Gem with a `DeathTreasureType` rolls its loot when used (`Gem.HandleUseCreateLoot`), at the Gem's Tier and LootQualityMod, rolling again until the item requires its `UseCreateLootAttribute` (PropertyInt 539), since the tables hold a few strays (a Swordstaff among the spears, Shoes among the Amuli armor). If none comes up in 20 rolls the crate is kept. Each crate opened is logged at Debug level (`[LOOT CRATE]`) with its tier and quality.
  - **Trade notes** (1057582, 1057592, 1057593), listed first: boxes of a 1,000, 5,000 or 10,000 note at 1 token per 1,000, opened by using them.
  - **Crafting supplies** (1057583-1057591), a little under their Value and open into the pack as soon as they're bought (a Gem with `OpensOnPurchase`, PropertyBool 187, used by `Player.FinalizeBuyTransaction`; with no room it stays in the pack to be used later; each one's description ends with its contents' own, so it can be read in the vendor window): Stack of 10 Pearls of Spell Transference 8, Stack of 25 Pearls of Spell Purging 4, Stack of 500 Alkahest Salt 4, Stack of 10 Bezel Fragments 8, a Scouring Stone 10, an Armor Style Kit 8, a Tailoring Kit 8, Stack of 10 Upgrade Kits 8, a Sanguine Crystal 4.
  - **Icons:** a trade note box shows its note's icon over the box, and crafting supplies have their contents' own icon, with a coloured underlay on the look-alike style and tailoring kits. Crates and writs show their tier as a digit (the Dungeon Mod difficulty overlays, 0x06006C34 + tier - 1; the tier 0 writ has none), and crates have an icon for their class: Warrior 0x06009011, Rogue 0x06009012, Caster 0x06009013, on the usual misc item background. Crates and writs also have the magic item glow (UiEffects).
  - **Held back for now:** the level 75-99 Writ of Gratitude (1057560) and the tier 6 and 7 Loot Crates (1057576-1057581) are in the database but not on his list, so he sells 34 of the 41 items. Until they return, a player of level 75 or above has no writ they can use.
- A held town with a Captain of the Guard standing near its centre (300 m) hands the rewards out from the Captain: each defender is told to claim theirs within 30 minutes. Claims are held in memory, so a restart in that half hour loses them. A town without a Captain yet, and a failed defence, pay at once.

## The Supply Chest

A town's Supply Chest stands by its Captain (world-db, Template `Town Supply Chest`; `TownSupplyChest`). Holtburg's is 2020059.

- **Chest raids.** When the town has a chest, every attack goes for it, for now (`ChestRaidChance`): an attack without an objective stops at the town centre once its road runs out. The scouts' warning says so, and its attackers march on from the town centre to the chest. Only the races that would raid a town for its supplies go for it (`TownCreaturePool.HumanoidTypes`: people, the tool-using races such as drudges, banderlings, mosswarts, lugians and tumeroks, and the undead); a town whose pool has none of them gets an ordinary attack instead.
- **Looting.** One attacker at a time loots the chest: the nearest one within 4 m of it with no one to fight. It turns to the chest and rummages in it every 3 seconds (the Pickup motion, or crouching for a creature whose motion table has none). Once attackers have spent 60 seconds at it between them, it's looted. A Town Defense member, guard or the Captain within 12 m of an idle attacker at the chest pulls it off to fight (`TownEvent.TickChest`, checked twice a second), and a free attacker takes over once none are near; progress is kept. The region is told when they reach it, at 25%, 50% and 75% emptied, when they're driven off (no one has looted for 3 seconds), and when they're back at it.
- **Looted.** The attack fails (a quarter of the XP), the town's supply goes to 0, so every guard upgrade ends at once, and the town's next unrest threshold is rolled at double.

## The Captain of the Guard

An NPC whose `Template` is `Captain of the Guard`, handled in C# (`TownCaptain`) the way the Market Broker is. Its town is the one nearest it.

Every town with guards has one, with a Supply Chest (2020059) beside it. Holtburg's (2020058) was placed by hand. The others (each town's guard WCID block, base+9) are made and placed by world-db's `scripts/town-guards/town_captains.py`. Each takes its town's guards' level, attributes, heritage and armor (the kit and its plain chainmail, without the helmet so the face shows), wields a two-handed sword of its heritage (Aluvian Greatsword 1057530, Sho Nodachi 1057531, Gharu'ndim Shashqa 1057532: copies made like the guards' weapons, damage 15), and stands outside the door of the smith's or tavern's building nearest the town centre (in Plateau Village and Stonehold, the shop's). Cragstone's centre in `Town.cs` had its latitude's sign wrong (26.0 S instead of N), so its guards, Captain and roads weren't found; it's fixed.

- **Use or talk:** pays the player's waiting reward, if they have one. Otherwise it first says how unsettled the town is, in words and without numbers (`TownEventManager.DescribeUnrestToPlayer`): from "All is quiet" through four steps of its share of the threshold (below 25%, 50%, 75% and 100%) to "An attack could come at any time" past it, or that it's under attack, or that the raiders have pulled back during its cooldown. With `town_events_enabled` off it never says an attack could come. Then it reports the town's supply in words (`TownSupply.DescribeLevel`), since players never hear the number: bare at level 0, then threadbare, meager, sparse, modest, adequate, sound, ample, plentiful, abundant and overflowing at level 10. It goes on with how far it is to the next, the loss an hour, how long the level lasts, and what's left of the player's donor buff. Saying anything with "claim" in it asks for the reward only.
- **Fights:** during an attack the Captain goes after the nearest attacker within 30 m of its post, keeps on it until it dies or gets 45 m from the post, then takes the next, and walks back to its post (FakeTeleport after 30 seconds) once there are none. The attack picks the targets (`TownEvent.CaptainFight`), so it only ever fights attackers, never players. It needs to be able to move (no `AiImmobile`).
- **Dressed like the guards:** `TownGuardUpgrades` dresses the Captain with the guards, so its worn armor counts at the same level, its kit and sword take the supply level's colours, and its sword gets the guards' weapon spells. It keeps its own archetype: 5 × toughness, 2 × lethality.
- **Can be attacked:** like the guards, it's `Attackable` with the guards' `Faction1Bits` (so monsters fight it) and `Tolerance` Monster (so it only fights monsters), and players can't hurt it (RubberGlue). Out of an attack it picks no fights of its own (`Creature.IsTownCaptain`), and stays at its post. When it dies during an attack the region is told, it drops out of the Town Defense panel, and a held town's rewards are paid at once instead of from the Captain. A linkable generator (7924, 5 minutes) brings it back where it stood, and the chest likewise.
- **Give:** pyreals and trade notes, any amount, go to the town's supply at face value. Anything else is handed back. Donations are never refused.
- **The donor buff, Guard's Gratitude** (spell 6864, category 865, in the client's portal.dat; world-db `spells/Other/06864 Guard's Gratitude.sql`): +10% XP for an hour (GlobalXpMod 0.1, additive, like Explorer Inspiration), with an icon. A player in the town's own level band (Holtburg's is 1 to 9, a tier 1 town's 10 to 19, and so on; higher and lower players both miss out) earns it by giving level 1's whole cost (500 pyreals at tier 0, 1,000 × tier² after). Gifts add up, in pyreals (`TownDonorProgress`, PropertyFloat 20057, saved): once what a player has given reaches the minimum of the town they give in, the Captain casts it and the count starts again from 0. A higher tier's minimum is dearer, so 250 given in Holtburg is 250 toward it, not half. The Captain says how far along a player is, after a gift and when talked to. Another earned buff tops it back up to an hour; it doesn't stack.

## Fellowship XP, for every fellowship

- The within-25-levels rule is gone. Shrouded fellows are left out of the level spread, and never receive XP from the others' kills; their own kills still share, and a Shrouded killer keeps their own share, cut by the usual overlevel penalty.
- The even share is 30% each at 10 fellows; past 10, the 300% is split evenly.

## Guard supply

Each town has a guard supply, which sets its supply level (0 to 10). The code is `TownSupply`.

- **Levels.** Reaching level L takes `B × (1.1^L − 1) / 0.1` supply, rounded to the nearest 50 pyreals, where B is level 1's cost: 500 at tier 0 and 1,000 × tier² from tier 1 (1,000, 4,000, 9,000, 16,000, 25,000). So level 1 is B, and each level after costs 10% more than the one before (`LevelGrowth`).
- **Drain.** Every minute the supply loses 1/60 of an hourly loss, a share of the current level's threshold: 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 10, 12, 15 and 40% for levels 1–10. Supply below level 1 drains at level 1's rate. The drain only counts time the server is up.
- **Minimum hold.** A level reached by adding supply holds for at least 15 minutes. `@townevent supply` sets the supply with no hold.
- **What a level does.** Each level takes 5% off the town's unrest threshold, so level 10 halves it: a full coffer draws more raids, which gives players a reason to donate. It also adds 10% to the wave budget and the rewards (`ThresholdReductionPerLevel`, `WaveBudgetPerLevel`, `RewardPerLevel`; the amounts are placeholders). They are read when used, so a level lost mid-attack counts at once.
- **Saving.** Every town's supply and hold is kept as JSON in `town_events_supply`, saved with the other properties every 5 minutes and at shutdown.

## Guards

While a town is under attack its guards see attackers from 60 m and run everywhere. A guard or attacker that gets no closer to what it's chasing for 7 seconds blips next to it, and an attacker stuck on its way in for 8 seconds blips onto its next waypoint.

Guards and the Captain are faction mobs, which go after any monster outside their faction. They leave alone harmless ones, any monster that doesn't attack on sight (Tolerance NoAttack, Appraise or Retaliate, such as chickens), unless it attacked them (`Creature.FactionMobIgnores`). This applies to every faction mob. The Captain goes further and never hunts on its own (`Creature.IsTownCaptain`): off duty it picks no targets and isn't woken by passing monsters, so it leaves the town's mice and hens alone too; it fights only the attackers an attack hands it.

A guard without the chainmail set keeps its kit.

**Armor and damage.** A guard's worn armor counts (`CreatureArmorEffectsDamageReduction`, which monsters otherwise lack), and each body part takes the higher of its archetype body armor and its worn armor (`ArmorTakesHigher`, guards only; other monsters with the flag still add the two). Every worn piece is set to the chainmail armor level for the town's tier, times its colour: chainmail 1, bronze 1.33, bright silver 1.67, gold 2, so gold is the top of the tier's loot range. Its ward (set by the archetype system, which ignores worn pieces) is scaled by the same amount. The weapon's damage rises with its colour: black 1, bronze 1.15, silver 1.3, gold 1.5.

## The event log

Every attack is saved when it ends, to the shard database (`TownEventRecorder`; tables from `2026-10-26-00-Town-Event-Log.sql` , `2026-10-28-00-Town-Event-Defenders.sql` and `2026-10-28-01-Town-Event-Combat.sql`). The writes run off the world thread, and a failed write is logged once.

- **`town_event`**, one row per attack:
  - **What and how:** the town, its tier, how it started (`unrest` or `admin`) and its outcome (`held`, `fell`, `looted`, `stopped` or `cancelled`).
  - **The attackers:** the creature families, the roads, whether it was a chest raid and how much was looted, and how many attackers spawned.
  - **The town:** its supply level at the start and the end, and the wave it reached of how many.
  - **The defenders:** how many earned credit and how many were rewarded, and the Warlord's pyreals each and how many were paid.
  - **Guards and players:** the damage dealt to attackers by the players (members at the town's level, and their pets), the guards and the Captain, the attackers each killed (a kill that doesn't count isn't here; `other_kills` are by anything else, or with no damager known), and who killed the Warlord. `@townevent status` and `@townevent history` show the split, and the server log has it when an attack ends. It is for balancing: guards should never hold off an attack on their own.
  - **Combat log** (`combat`, JSON; `TownCombatStats`): both ways between the raiders and the players, guards and Captain, every melee and missile swing (how many were evaded, blocked or parried, and the landed ones' damage before and after armor, resistances and shields) and all the damage dealt, spells included. Status, history and the end-of-attack log line show it as one line per pairing.
  - **When:** start and end times, and the duration.
- **`town_event_participant`**, one row per player who was scored:
  - **Who:** their level, and whether they were still in the Town Defense at the end.
  - **Scores:** damage, healing, damage taken and killing blows, with their rank in each among those who earned credit (1 is top, ties share), and how many categories they topped.
  - **The over-level log:** over-level kills, prompts refused, kills after refusing, and lost credit.
  - **Rewards:** the reward (`paid`, `claim`, `claimed`, `expired` or `none`), its multiplier, the XP (before the player's own XP bonuses), the Warlord's pyreals, and when it was claimed.

A reward waiting at the Captain updates its row when it's claimed or runs out.
